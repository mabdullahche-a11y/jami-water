How to read a TDS number

What a handheld meter tells you about dissolved solids.

Read the number in context

Total dissolved solids (TDS) describes the amount of material dissolved in water, often reported in milligrams per litre. A handheld meter estimates it from electrical conductivity. Temperature and the mixture of salts can affect that estimate.

Lower is not automatically better

Saiqa Imran of PCRWR’s National Water Quality Laboratory told APP that roughly 300 to 500 mg/L can provide a more desirable mineral content. That is context about minerals, rather than a treatment target or proof of safety.

Check the dated report

A handheld TDS meter cannot identify arsenic or tell you whether a sample contains E. coli. Match the report to your batch and compare each measured result with the applicable limit.
